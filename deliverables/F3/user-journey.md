# User Journey

Lets say that Ash is a busy student who likes to draw but finds it difficult to carve out practice time to improve his skills in a structured, accountable way. He can use Redraw to make this process easier.

On opening the site, Ash is prompted to log in or register. He can register (so the site remembers who he is next time) and is brought to a main dashboard with a helpful informational overlay explaining what Redraw is, who it’s for and how it’s used. He can close that informational popup and view it anytime later by going to the help page.

On the dashboard, Ash can see a weekly practice streak counter currently at 0, a big practice button, a list of art fundamentals with measures of how much they’ve been practiced, and a feed of recently posted artwork. If he wants, he can follow the artists on the feed to have their work appear first in the future. He can also find friends to follow by using a search bar on the page and searching by username. There’s also an option to filter his feed by which art fundamental the reference was tagged with.

On pressing the profile button, Ash is brought to the profile page, which has a GitHub-style frequency grid showing his practice as well as other analytics. He can also see which other users he follows, and which users follow him. Ash can click back to the dashboard to return to it.

On pressing the big practice button, Ash is given the list of fundamentals to choose from, with a randomized option and a suggestion based on his practice history. After choosing a fundamental, he chooses from a small number of fundamental-tagged references, and is brought to the drawing interface.

On entering the drawing interface, Ash is shown simple drawing tools, the reference image he chose, and a timer for 30 minutes. He can draw however he likes until the timer runs out, at which point he is given 1 minute to wrap up before being brought to the next page.

After the drawing timer ends, Ash is shown his drawing and a timelapse of him making it, alongside the reference and options to post, save, or delete his work. Regardless of which he picks, his streak counter and analytics records update to reflect his recent practice. On the same page, he can also see other people’s posted drawings and timelapses done from the same reference image. When he’s selected one of the three options, he’s returned to the main dashboard, having successfully made time to practice for 30 minutes.
