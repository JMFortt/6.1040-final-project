# How to innovate from bad situations

Original: https://61040.github.io/fa26/background/innovating-from-bad-situations/

## Do not start from

- A technology (“we should use X”)
- A vague value (“simple,” “engaging,” “scalable”)
- A user-stated want or a named feature (“add emoji reactions”)

Wants are imagined solutions. People describe problems more reliably than products.

## Start from a bad situation

Something that already happens: a person cannot do what they mean to, or something they dislike keeps occurring. Confirm it, make it concrete, give it a cost, and check that a design can change it. Recurring costs beat rare trivial ones. Rare catastrophes still count.

“Cannot do X” is often a feature request. Ask what goes wrong because X is missing.

Each distinct bad thing tends to become its own concept. The iPod’s “cannot take all your music,” “painful to obtain files,” and “must buy the whole album” were different mitigations.

## Purpose line

Keep the bad situation next to the purpose (a “prevents” clause). If it is missing, the purpose is probably too thin to judge.

## When asked for a feature

Restate it as a bad situation before writing state and actions. If that restatement is not in the design, ask. Do not invent the situation in order to justify the feature.
