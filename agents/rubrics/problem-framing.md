# Rubric: problem framing

Original: https://61040.github.io/fa26/rubrics/problem-framing/

Ethical analysis is an extra requirement of the final project. It is described in `../assignment.md`, not here.

## Required parts

- **Domain** — enough background for a stranger; focused on what the bad situations need; includes why people do those activities
- **Bad situations** — concrete scenarios (lead-up, pain, consequences); cost and frequency; not a UI
- **Corroboration** — evidence tied to those situations; fair about how strong the source is
- **Workarounds and comparables** — why current workarounds hurt; the major kinds of existing tools and why they miss *these* situations
- **Solution sketch** — not a full design; evidence the problem is tractable; graspable by a layperson

## Pitfalls

Believing the unhappiness and also believing the user’s hypothesized fix. Domain too broad. Vague bad situation. A feature written as if it were the problem. Cherry-picked evidence. Low impact. Comparables that do not address the situations. A sketch that restates the problem, lists unfocused features, is infeasible, or requires people to change how they live.

## Levels

- **Deficient** — missing parts, unintelligible, or only a feature list
- **Emergent** — domain is ok and a situation is believable, but too thin to judge solutions; weak workarounds or comparables; sketch vague or implausible
- **Competent** — all parts clear; costs clear; context assessed; sketch realistic. The situations may still be less than compelling, or existing workarounds may look good enough
- **Expert** — an “aha”: a bad situation people tolerated without seeing it as a hole
